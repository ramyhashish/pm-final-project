# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A three-year delivery driver who completes dozens of stops a day, often in bad weather with his hands full (UXR-01).
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Confirm a delivery and receive route changes quickly, with minimal interaction with the app.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Marking a stop delivered takes three taps across three screens (BUG-2055), so at a rainy doorstep with a package in hand he “just texts” his dispatcher instead, creating the off-platform coordination your hook describes.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Text messages to the dispatcher (documented): Diego’s substitute for the in-app “Mark delivered” action.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Phone calls to the office (documented for other drivers): Elena called the office when the app crashed (UXR-03). This is the likely fallback when a text isn’t enough, though it isn’t documented for Diego.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** WhatsApp group (documented at the dispatcher level): Dispatchers describe a WhatsApp group as “the real system” (UXR-02). If Diego’s texts land there, it is the same channel, but the research doesn’t confirm this.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Paper manifest (documented at the focus group level): Five of seven drivers keep one “just in case the app dies” (UXR-12). Not confirmed for Diego specifically.
